      How this was measured, and what it can't tell you

      Blocks
      Each line on the map is one stretch of street between two intersections, as San Francisco Public Works maps it, and is named by its hundred block, from the house numbers along it. Every other dataset on this page is joined to these stretches by the city's centerline number (CNN). Freeways and their ramps, private roads, and streets that exist only on paper are left out.
      Data
      SFMTA parking citations from the last two years, snapped to the nearest block side by their coordinates; the street sweeping schedule, parking regulations, meters and meter policies, all from data.sf.gov.
      Can I park here?
      Worked out in your browser from the posted rules for each block side, for the time and stay you pick. It can't see temporary signs that aren't in the city's data.
      Limits
      A ticket only appears when someone broke the rule, so quiet blocks look less enforced than they are. Always read the posted signs.
      Blocks
      Each line on the map is one stretch of street between two intersections, as San Francisco Public Works maps it, and is named by its hundred block, from the house numbers along it. Every other dataset on this page is joined to these stretches by the city's centerline number (CNN). Freeways and their ramps, private roads, and streets that exist only on paper are left out.
      Data
      Traffic crashes resulting in injury or death (SFPD and SFDPH, via data.sf.gov) from the last five years where a pedestrian was involved; the 2024 High Injury Network; signals, crosswalks, safety zones, speed limits and traffic calming.
      Limits
      Only reported crashes with an injury are counted, and the data runs about two months behind. A street with few crashes may just have few people walking on it; counts aren't adjusted for foot traffic.

      About This page

      Made by Citina Liang, after LA Street Rules. All from San Francisco's open data.

      Not a guide to parking illegally, and not a promise of safety. This page shows the city's posted rules, the tickets it has written, and the crashes it has recorded. Past tickets and crashes don't predict the next one.

      What the page loads. The map images come from OpenStreetMap and the block data from this site, one small area at a time, so both can tell roughly which part of the city you're looking at. If you use your location, it only moves the map; the exact spot isn't sent anywhere.
